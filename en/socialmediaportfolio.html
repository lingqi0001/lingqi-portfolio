How does comparing your own lives to idealized posts on social media affect teenagers’ self-esteem and depressive symptoms?

Independent Academic Research Paper

Background of the Research Issue

Teenagers’ depression rates have surged by over 50% since 2010. Social media networks often leave teens feeling isolated and inadequate.

Social media acts as a "highlight reel." People only show their best lives instead of what’s actually happening. Teens measure their messy, real-life struggles against someone else’s polished, carefully selected moments.

This is known as upward comparison. It triggers persistent feelings of inferiority. Social media's intense obsession with peer perfection prevents genuine, horizontal peer connection, substituting authentic community with visual hierarchy.

Furthermore, recommendation algorithms push the most perfect, engaging, and filtered posts to the top of feed cycles. Digital filters construct a "fake reality" that is impossible to emulate. Protecting adolescent mental health calls for increasing media literacy and supportive adult guidance.

Research Methodology & Methodology Overview

This research utilized a dual-method approach. Secondary research analyzed existing psychological literature regarding social comparison and algorithm structures. Primary research consisted of semi-structured interviews with five experts in psychology and social media technology.

About the Researcher

I am Lingqi Mo. I conducted this independent research to understand why social media comparison is damaging to adolescent mental health. During this project, I learned that social media platforms are engineered to capture attention at the cost of self-esteem. Recognizing these online portrayals are not reality is the first step to breaking the cycle.
